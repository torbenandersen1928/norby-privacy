Nørby · for testers

Your baking stays yours.

Thank you for testing Nørby. Everything you make in the app stays on your phone, with a copy in your own iCloud. I don't receive any of it.

Nørby has no account and no sign-in, shows no ads, doesn't track you, and only goes online to keep your own iCloud copy up to date. Nothing in the app sends anything to me or to anyone else.

Who I am

Nørby is made by me, Torben Andersen, in the UK. It isn't a company yet. While you're testing, I'm the person responsible for any information about you. The law calls this the "data controller".

Questions, worries, or bread talk: norbybakes@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

All of it:

  • your loaves, recipes and methods
  • your bakes, scorecards and notes
  • your plans, reminders and timers
  • your starters and their feeds
  • your name and photo on the You tab
  • every photo you take or choose

Photos are saved without the hidden location data your camera adds, so a picture of your loaf never says where your kitchen is.

What goes to your own iCloud

Nørby keeps a copy of everything above in your own private iCloud, so deleting the app or changing your phone doesn't lose your bakes.

That copy is stored by Apple, in your Apple Account, and counts towards your iCloud storage. I can't see it or open it. If you're not signed in to iCloud, or you switch iCloud off for Nørby in your iPhone's Settings, everything simply stays on the phone.

What the app asks permission for

  • Camera: only when you tap to photograph a loaf or your starter.
  • Notifications and alarms: for planning reminders and step timers. These are set up on your phone itself, not sent from anywhere.
  • The lock-screen countdown: so you can see a timer without opening the app.

Choosing a photo from your library needs no permission. Apple's photo picker gives Nørby only the photo you pick. You can change any of these in your iPhone's Settings.

What TestFlight shows me

Testing goes through Apple's TestFlight app, which Apple runs under its own privacy notice. Through it, Apple shows me:

  • your name and email address, if I invited you by email (not if you joined from a public link)
  • whether you've installed the test, how many times you've opened it, which version, your iPhone model, iOS version and language
  • a crash report, if Nørby crashes and your phone shares crash reports
  • anything you send me as feedback: your comment and screenshot, plus a few details Apple adds about your phone (such as battery level, free space and time zone)

I use this only to fix and improve Nørby. I never sell it, share it, or use it for advertising. I keep your email address and feedback until the test round ends, then delete them within three months unless you're still testing. Apple keeps feedback for up to a year under its own rules. Apple may handle this information outside the UK, including in the United States, under its own safeguards.

Why I'm allowed to use it: you've agreed to try an early version, and I have a fair, limited reason (the law calls it a "legitimate interest") to see whether it works for you.

Your choices

  • Ask me what I hold about you, ask me to correct it, or ask me to delete it. Email me and I'll reply within a month, usually much sooner.
  • Stop testing at any time: delete Nørby and leave the test in the TestFlight app.
  • If you're unhappy with how I've handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office. I'd be grateful for the chance to put it right first.

This test is for adults. I share it personally, by email invitation or a private TestFlight link, and the link is capped at a small number of testers.

When this changes

Later, Nørby will let you publish a bake, follow friends and gift a starter. Those will need a sign-in, and what you choose to publish will go to Nørby's server. I'll update this page and tell you before that arrives. Nothing will ever be published unless you press publish.
