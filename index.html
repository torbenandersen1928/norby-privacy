Nørby · privacy

Your baking stays yours.

Nørby is built to hold as little about you as possible. Everything you make stays on your phone, with a copy in your own iCloud that I can't see. Only what you choose to share goes to Nørby's server. And the app sends a few anonymous counts of how it's used, which you can switch off.

Nørby shows no ads, doesn't track you across other apps or websites, and never sells or shares your information for marketing. Baking needs no account.

What's in your test build today. The usage counts (section 4) and the iCloud copy (section 3) are in the test build you have now.

Accounts, sharing, following, starter gifts, likes and comments are arriving in the next updates. Sections 5 to 8 describe them so you know what's coming before it does. They apply to you from the day each one reaches your phone, and I'll say so in the app.

1. Who I am

Nørby is made by me, Torben Andersen, a sole trader in the UK. It isn't a company yet. I'm the person responsible for your information (the law calls this the "data controller"), and I'm registered with the Information Commissioner's Office.

Questions, worries, or bread talk: norbybakes@gmail.com.

2. What stays on your phone

All of this, and I never receive it:

  • your loaves, recipes and methods
  • your bakes, scorecard notes and private notes
  • your plans, reminders and timers
  • your starters and their feeds
  • every photo you take or choose, unless you share it (section 5)

Photos are saved without the hidden location data your camera adds, so a picture of your loaf never says where your kitchen is.

Permissions. The camera is used only when you tap to photograph a loaf or your starter. Notifications and alarms are for planning reminders and step timers, and are set up on your phone itself. Choosing a photo from your library needs no permission: Apple's photo picker gives Nørby only the photo you pick. You can change any of these in your iPhone's Settings.

3. What goes to your own iCloud

Nørby keeps a copy of everything above in your own private iCloud, so deleting the app or changing phone doesn't lose your bakes.

That copy is stored by Apple, in your Apple Account, and counts towards your iCloud storage. I can't see it or open it. If you're not signed in to iCloud, or you switch iCloud off for Nørby in your iPhone's Settings, everything simply stays on the phone.

4. Anonymous usage counts

This is the one thing Nørby sends from your phone without you pressing "share". It's switched on to begin with, and you can switch it off any time.

What's counted

When you do something in Nørby, the app sends a short note that it happened: the app was opened, a loaf was made, a bake was started or finished, a scorecard was saved, a starter was made or fed, a day was planned. A finished bake also carries a few numbers: whether it was a full bake or a quick photo post, whether you shared it, added a crumb photo or scored it, and, for a full bake, how many hours it took, the dough's hydration rounded to the nearest 5%, how many steps you ticked out of how many there were, and which bake of that loaf it was. Each note also says which version of Nørby you have, how many days since you first opened it, and whether it's a test copy.

What's never sent

No recipes, loaf or starter names, captions, notes, photos, your name, your email address or your location. The app has no way to put words into these counts.

Why

To see whether people keep baking with Nørby and where they get stuck, so I can fix the right things. I don't use the counts for advertising, and I never sell them.

Who holds them

PostHog, a company that makes analytics software, on its servers in the EU (Frankfurt). It handles the counts on my behalf and can't use them for its own purposes. It's set to keep only these notes: no screen recording, no automatic capture of taps, and no working out where you are from your internet address. To receive the counts, PostHog necessarily sees your phone's internet address; I've asked it not to store it.

Signed in or not

Using Nørby never needs an account, and the counts are recorded either way.

  • If you don't sign in, each copy of Nørby has a random number that doesn't come from your name, email or phone. The counts are kept against that number only, so they aren't tied to who you are.
  • If you sign in to a Nørby account (once accounts arrive), that number is joined to your account's reference number, never your email address. The counts from that phone, including ones sent before you signed in, then belong to your account, so one baker on two phones counts once. If you sign out, the phone starts again with a new random number.

Your past bakes

The first time you open a version that has counts, the bakes already on your phone are counted once: the same few numbers, dated when each bake was finished, and marked as old. No words or photos.

Where did you hear about Nørby?

When you first sign in, I may ask this once, with a list of answers to tap. You can skip it. If you answer, the answer is counted the same way.

How long

Up to a year. PostHog then deletes them.

Your choice

In the app: You → Settings → Help improve Nørby. Switch it off and nothing more is sent, and anything waiting to be sent is thrown away. Counts already sent stay until the year is up. Because they're not tied to your name, I can't tell which are yours unless you were signed in. If you were, ask me and I'll delete them.

Why I'm allowed

These are statistics about how the app is used. UK law lets me collect that kind of statistic without asking first, as long as I tell you clearly and you can switch it off. This page and the Settings switch do both. The legal reason for handling the counts is a fair, limited one (the law calls it a "legitimate interest"): I need to know whether the app works for bakers.

6. Why I'm allowed to use it

  • Your account and everything you share: you asked for an account and chose to share, so I need this to give you that (the law calls this "contract").
  • Blocks, reports, the record of the terms you agreed, the logs that stop guessing, and the usage counts: a fair, limited reason to keep bakers and Nørby safe, and to see whether the app works ("legitimate interest").
  • If the law ever requires me to keep or hand over something ("legal obligation"). I'd only do that when it's genuinely required.

7. Reports, blocking and what I can see

You can report a bake, a comment or a profile, and block a baker. Blocking works both ways: neither of you sees the other's profile, bakes, likes or comments, and they aren't told.

  • When someone reports something, I get an email within a minute saying what kind of thing was reported, with a link to my dashboard. The email never contains the reported words or photo, or the name of the person who reported. It goes to the Nørby email address above and is sent through Resend (section 9).
  • While a report is open, I keep what was reported, including the bake's photos and the comment's words, even if the baker then unshares it, changes it or deletes their account. That's so what was reported can't be removed before I've looked at it. Nobody else can see it.
  • When I've dealt with a report, that kept evidence is deleted. The record of the report itself (what kind it was, the reporter's optional note, and what I did) is kept for 12 months, then deleted. It's there so I can tell if someone is reported more than once.
  • I'm the only person who reads reports, and I act on them within 24 hours. I can take content down, warn a baker or remove an account.

8. How long it's kept, and deleting your account

  • What you share is kept until you remove it. Stop sharing a bake and it goes from the server at once, together with its photos, likes and comments. Change your name or photo and the old one is replaced. A comment can't be edited: the writer can delete it (or the baker, on their own bake), then post again.
  • Delete your account (You → Settings → Delete my account) and the server removes, in one step: your profile and photo, every bake you shared and its photos, your likes and comments, your follows (both ways), your gifts and requests, your blocks, the reports you made, your starters on family trees that no other starter came from, the record of the terms, and your sign-in. If you signed in with Apple, Apple is told to forget Nørby too. The usage counts attached to your account are deleted if you ask me.
  • What stays: a starter that other bakers' starters came from stays on its tree with its name, kept by "a baker", with nothing to link it to you. Anything under an open report stays until I've dealt with it (section 7). Everything on your phone stays on your phone.
  • Backups. Every week I make a copy of the server's data on my own computer, kept off iCloud, for four weeks and then deleted. Anything you delete disappears from those copies within four weeks. Photos aren't in these copies.
  • Usage counts: up to a year (section 4).

10. What TestFlight shows me

Testing goes through Apple's TestFlight app, which Apple runs under its own privacy notice. Through it, Apple shows me:

  • your name and email address, if I invited you by email (not if you joined from a public link)
  • whether you've installed the test, how many times you've opened it, which version, your iPhone model, iOS version and language
  • a crash report, if Nørby crashes and your phone shares crash reports
  • anything you send me as feedback: your comment and screenshot, plus a few details Apple adds about your phone (such as battery level, free space and time zone)

I use this only to fix and improve Nørby. I never sell it, share it, or use it for advertising. I keep your email address and feedback until the test round ends, then delete them within three months unless you're still testing. Apple keeps feedback for up to a year under its own rules. Apple may handle this information outside the UK, including in the United States, under its own safeguards.

11. Who Nørby is for

Nørby accounts are for adults: you must be 18 or over, and you confirm that when you sign up. If I learn that someone under 18 has an account, I'll delete it. The test itself is for adults I've invited. I share it personally, by email invitation or a private TestFlight link, and the link is capped at a small number of testers.

12. Your rights

You can ask me:

  • what I hold about you, and for a copy you can take elsewhere
  • to correct it (you can change your name and photo yourself)
  • to delete it (once you have an account, you can do this yourself in the app)
  • to stop or limit something, or to object to it

Email me at norbybakes@gmail.com and I'll reply within a month, usually much sooner. You can stop testing at any time: delete Nørby and leave the test in the TestFlight app. If you're unhappy with how I've handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office. I'd be grateful for the chance to put it right first.

13. Keeping it safe

Shared photos are kept privately, not at public web addresses, and load only for signed-in bakers who are allowed to see that bake. Every part of the server refuses by default and opens only what's described here, and independent reviews of it were done and their findings fixed. Passwords are never stored in a readable form. Your sign-in on the phone is kept in the iPhone's Keychain. The app only ever holds a key that lets it send and ask, never one that lets it do more. If something ever went wrong and put your information at risk, I'd tell the Information Commissioner's Office within 72 hours, and tell you if the risk to you is high.

14. When this changes

I'll update this page, and tell you in the app, before anything changes what Nørby holds or sends. Nothing is ever shared unless you share it.
